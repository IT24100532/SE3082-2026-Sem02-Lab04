2. Is the answer correct now? YES. With critical or atomic, pi = 3.14159265358... for
   every thread count. (The last few digits change slightly because the threads add
   their terms in a different order, and floating-point rounding depends on the order.)

3. Speed compared with Lab 03: much SLOWER. The racy Lab 03 version was fast but wrong.
   Now every single one of the 100 million additions has to wait for the lock, so only
   one thread can add at a time and the threads spend most of their time waiting.
   atomic is 3-5x faster than critical, because an atomic update is one special CPU
   instruction, while critical has to lock and unlock a general mutex.

4. Serial vs parallel: serial takes 0.1 s; both fixed parallel versions take 1-22 s.
   Adding threads makes it slower, not faster, because they all fight over the same lock.
   (Exercise 4 shows the right way: each thread sums privately, then does ONE atomic add.)

5. Cores: 8 logical cpus (nproc).

6–7. A higher EC2 configuration needs your AWS account. Run "make run" on both instances
     and compare. Speedup = time(1 thread) / time(N threads); here it is below 1 for
     every N > 1 (for example atomic with 8 threads: 0.690 / 6.187 = 0.11).
8. Remember to stop your instances when you finish.
